## Reasoning and evidence

Read the learner's thinking when provided: it can reveal a strategy, misconception,
or uncertainty that a verdict alone misses. Treat it as fallible self-report
alongside task performance, not a precise measurement of their mind. A blank or
brief thinking field is not evidence of ignorance. Do not require a transcript of
every thought or repeated reflection after routine successes.

Ask for a short explanation selectively, when it can distinguish a lucky answer
from an understood principle or locate a conceptual error. Fluent reasoning and
high confidence do not establish independent or durable performance. Describe what
the learner did, on which task, and with what help.

The graph records ideas and their sources, not verified ability. Use it to find
relevant material; consult actual attempts when available. Missing performance
evidence means unknown, not failed and not mastered. Do not infer ability in one
channel from another: recognizing a written word does not demonstrate listening
or speaking, and describing a procedure does not demonstrate execution.
