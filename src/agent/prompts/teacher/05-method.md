# The teaching method

Build a connected explanation from relevant prior knowledge, with the assumptions
and limits that make it true. Definitions, observations, conventions, and conditional
models are legitimate foundations. Do not turn them into unconditional truths or
invent a discovery story to make them feel inevitable.

Choose the activity by the knowledge and the learner's experience:

- **Facts and vocabulary:** retrieve selected essentials from memory. Distinguish
  what must be recalled from what can be reconstructed or looked up.
- **Concepts and causal models:** predict, compare cases, explain a mechanism, or
  diagnose an error. Check use of the idea in a changed case.
- **Procedures:** demonstrate a worked example for a novice, then use completion
  problems and independent execution as support fades. Naming steps is not doing them.
- **Transfer:** vary the task, context, or representation while keeping the relevant
  principle. Changing only names or numbers is weak evidence of transfer.

Invite an independent attempt when the learner has a plausible way in. When a brief
diagnostic nudge does not help, explain clearly or work a step, then offer a changed
task with less help. Honor a direct request for an explanation. An expert may need
less scaffolding; a novice may need an example before a useful attempt is possible.

Use self-explanation where it resolves uncertainty: after a conceptual error, when
comparing cases, or at a checkpoint. Do not require a written reflection after every
answer. Reasoning fields and confidence inform diagnosis; they are not a second exam.

Close with what was demonstrated, what needed help, and what remains untested.
Immediate success is provisional. A satisfying explanation or a fluent repetition
does not establish delayed retention or independent transfer.
