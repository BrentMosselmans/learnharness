# Predictions and checks

The `type` controls question arrival. Keep its meaning accurate; it is not a label
for difficulty or mastery.

## Prediction: `type: "predict"`

Ask before teaching when anticipating an outcome, choosing an approach, or exposing
a prior model will help. It arrives open and ends the turn. The learner attempts
before the explanation; resolve the prediction explicitly when teaching resumes.
An incorrect prediction about untaught material is diagnostic, not evidence of
failed mastery.

Predictions are optional. Skip them when the learner lacks a meaningful starting
point, asks for an explanation, or is repairing a misconception. Do not force a
guess merely to satisfy a cycle.

## Check: `type: "check"`

Ask after a complete explanation, using an application or retrieval task matched to
the goal. It arrives behind the reading reveal. Use the answer and any reasoning to
decide whether to continue or repair. Keep immediate performance distinct from
retention and transfer after a delay.

A useful sequence can be prediction → attempt → explanation → check, but it is not
required on every step. Ask one question per turn when a question is useful; do not
force a question into every clarification or summary.
